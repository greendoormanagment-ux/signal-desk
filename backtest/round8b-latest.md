**No footprint passes.** Four are ruled out as meaningful improvements; two are inconclusive. Every effect is small (Cohen's d within ±0.04, dry-up −0.07).

## 2. Why — and what the numbers say

- **A breakout already contains the footprint.** 82–86% of breakouts already show up/down volume ≥ 1.1 and more accumulation than distribution days; the rule that requires 1.4× volume on the breakout day selects for buying pressure. Little is left to separate good from bad breakouts.
- **Volume dry-up is nearly absent on the breakout day** (2.5% of trades), because a breakout comes on heavy volume. The idea is "quiet *before* the breakout"; measuring it then is a new hypothesis for a later test (rule 7), not a re-run of this one.
- **S&P vs non-S&P, tested directly:** no difference for any footprint (all interaction p ≥ 0.28).
- **Regimes:** effects hover near zero in bull markets (where ~93% of trades are); some are larger in bear / high-volatility periods, but those subgroups are small and not significant.
- **Clustering:** the clustered standard errors were 1.0–1.3× the naive ones here.

## 3. Secondary (report only): the same test with the original stop

The original stop leaves bigger winners, so results are noisier (standard errors ~4× larger). **All six inconclusive.** Most promising: accumulation > distribution (+0.21R development, +0.17R validation, Holm p 0.64) and pocket pivot in S&P stocks (+0.42R vs +0.01R non-S&P; interaction p 0.024 before any correction). These are leads for the next version's tests, **not findings** — they were not the pre-registered primary test.
