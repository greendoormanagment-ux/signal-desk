It is not one or two lucky stocks — removing the top 10 still leaves +0.45R — but it *is* a handful of big moves per year. Missing them, or selling them early, would erase the edge.

All signals average about the same (+0.67R vs +0.62R), but **the trades that win a slot are very different** (+2.34R vs +0.43R). Non-S&P stocks flood the slots with 3× as many signals and are held for shorter spells. **Which signals get the 10 slots is the biggest lever found so far.**

In development (2005–16) the two portfolios were close (S&P +1.6, non-S&P +1.9 pts vs SPY), so the S&P advantage is also period-dependent.

## 3. What it means

1. **No more filters.** Nine filters tested (6 footprints + 3 trend filters): none adds value on top of the breakout rule. Simpler is right.
2. **Never cut winners short, never skip signals in a losing streak.** The whole edge comes from a few large winners; a 60–150-trade losing streak is normal.
3. **Next lever: ranking signals for the 10 slots.** The data-driven score + odds (planned after Round 8) should be built and tested as a *ranking* tool — which signal to take when there are more signals than slots. That is where the measured difference is.
4. **S&P-only: not yet.** Strong in one period, absent in the other (as the colleague cautioned).
